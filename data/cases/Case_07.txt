2. CASE BACKGROUND
--------------------------------------------------

Kabir Malhotra purchased a smartphone on
18 March 2026 for Rs. 35,000.

After approximately two months, the phone allegedly
overheated while charging.

The claimant states that the battery expanded and
damaged the back cover of the device.

He contacted the seller and manufacturer and requested
a replacement and compensation.

The manufacturer arranged a product inspection.
However, the claimant disputes the conclusions
of the inspection.

4. CLAIMANT'S ARGUMENT
--------------------------------------------------

The claimant argues that:

1. The smartphone overheated during normal charging.

2. The battery expanded and damaged the device.

3. The product created a possible safety risk.

4. The manufacturer should be responsible for the
   alleged product defect.

5. The claimant requests replacement, refund and
   appropriate compensation.

The claimant's draft submission states:

"Every manufacturer is automatically liable for
all injuries or damage involving its product,
regardless of the cause."

IMPORTANT TEST DEFECT:
This is an overbroad legal proposition. Liability
must be examined according to applicable law and
the facts and evidence of the case.

--------------------------------------------------
5. RESPONDENT'S DEFENCE
--------------------------------------------------

The manufacturer argues that:

1. The inspection report identifies possible
   physical damage near the charging port.

2. The claimant may have used a third-party charger.

3. The cause of battery expansion has not been
   independently established.

4. The claimant has not supplied an independent
   battery safety report.

5. The manufacturer denies that the product had
   a manufacturing defect.

--------------------------------------------------
6. DISPUTED FACTS
--------------------------------------------------

1. Whether the phone overheated during normal use.

2. Whether the battery expansion resulted from
   a manufacturing defect.

3. Whether a third-party charger was used.

4. Whether the inspection report is reliable.

5. Whether the product caused any legally
   compensable harm.
